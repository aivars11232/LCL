LEARNED COMPUTING LANGUAGE (LCL)
TITLE: Validation, Execution, Failure, and Termination
LANGUAGE VERSION: 0.1.0
STATUS: NORMATIVE
DATE: 2026-09-05


All lexical, grammar, reference-name, static type, dependency, rule-conflict,
scope, and required VALIDATE checks complete before first side effect. Static
checking does not demand future OUTPUT values. Invalid source ends
status.invalid. Missing external data/capability can yield status.blocked.

status.blocked is terminal for that invocation and has no outgoing transition.
Later availability permits a separate explicit invocation with its own identity
and result; the prior blocked result remains evidence. A still-active parent
producer may recover a child's terminal result through its declared handler,
retry, or fallback contract without modifying that child's result. A non-root
declaration with FALSE applicability or optional omission transitions from
status.ready to status.skipped without beginning its operation. An execution
root cannot take this transition.

During execution, failure of a required action invokes applicable handler/retry;
otherwise status.failed. core.stop yields status.stopped unless another declared
failure status applies. Cancellation yields status.cancelled. Host limitations
produce error.host.constraint and never change LCL meaning.

Every exposed core-operation result records failure_phase and effect_state as
separate axes. failure_phase is none when no failure exists. pre_effect means
the producer failed before any concrete effect began; this includes a command
that could not start. post_effect means at least one effect began before the
failure. indeterminate is used only when available observations cannot establish
whether an effect began before failure. The selected error and status do not by
themselves infer one of these phases.

effect_state none means no concrete effect began, applied means every begun
authorized effect completed, partial means at least one begun authorized effect
did not complete, and indeterminate means the extent of effects cannot be
established. Every known begun effect is retained in observed_effects with its
own applied, partial, or indeterminate record state. The operation's resolved
possible-effect set bounds those records.

A pre-effect failure requires effect_state none, an empty observed_effects list,
and no bound or partial OUTPUT. A post-effect failure may have applied or partial
effects and may retain known schema-local effect-truth fields. Those fields are
observations, not implicit OUTPUT bindings. An indeterminate failure phase uses
effect_state indeterminate unless all available evidence proves the exact effect
state independently.

status.partial means only that the producer completed a declared subset of its
required work. It does not imply that OUTPUT is partial and does not imply that
effects are partial. Conversely, effect_state partial does not force
status.partial: the applicable failure, cancellation, stop, or block contract
determines status while the effect state records what happened.

The complete per-error phase metadata is resolved from
10_REGISTRIES/statuses_and_errors_v0.1.0.json. failure_phase is measured at the
exposed result producer. A child operation or retry attempt records its local
phase and effects in ordered evidence, while an aggregate producer accounts for
every effect begun within its whole invocation. The first unhandled diagnostic
in stable order supplies its resolved default_status; secondary diagnostics do
not override it. Recovery promotes the first remaining unhandled diagnostic to
primary. Only recovery of every applicable diagnostic or successful substitution
of a local unsuccessful primary/retry invocation permits the declared handler
result to control status. A substituted invocation's diagnostics remain local
evidence, and all its effects remain part of the aggregate effect record.
Substitution never turns invalid source into valid source. Phase does not alter
status. Error and status aliases resolve to canonical core identifiers before
these rules; aliases cannot change default_status, recoverability, or phase.

The exact expression_demand_resolution map in the diagnostic-selection registry
distinguishes preflight source checks from value-domain failures of statically
valid expressions actually demanded after preflight. Only its listed triggers
may resolve to execution stage and status.failed, with required MISSING and
UNKNOWN retaining status.blocked. The exact identifier and inherited event and
recoverability remain unchanged. Apply this resolution before suppression,
ordering, primary selection, and phase lookup. The execution-stage phase family
then accounts for earlier effects of the exposed producer. Unreachable or
short-circuited values are not demanded, and source structure, name, arity, and
static type defects can never be reclassified by this rule.

error.dependency.unsatisfied and error.scope.violation are pre_effect only:
dependency availability and effective scope resolve before the first authorized
effect. error.execution.order may be pre_effect at graph construction or the
first operation, post_effect after an earlier aggregate child effect, or
indeterminate when earlier effect timing is unknown. error.required.missing may
be pre_effect when absence is known before effects, post_effect when an item
becomes required or is found missing after an earlier aggregate effect, or
indeterminate when the timing cannot be established.

The other mixed-phase identifiers are error.cancelled,
error.evidence.missing, error.execution.action, error.host.constraint,
error.operation.postcondition, error.operation.precondition,
error.permission.denied, error.retry.exhausted, error.success.unsatisfied,
error.value.unknown, and error.verification.failed. Their exact triggers are in
the registry. An immediate operation precondition still precedes that immediate
operation's effects; its aggregate producer may have earlier child effects.
Likewise, a verification or completion failure after effect-free work remains
pre_effect, while one after known effects is post_effect.

Every failure record retains the canonical error identifier, registered stage,
resolved expression-demand stage when applicable, producer
path, canonical source locus or declared execution event, applicable iteration
and attempt indexes, and the observations supporting phase, effects, and OUTPUT
binding. The four baseline errors also retain the exact dependency state,
ordering edge and prior child results, missing-item detection point, or resolved
target/effective-scope relation specified by the registry. Absence of evidence
never proves absence of effects or OUTPUT.

Every ACTION that resolves exactly once remains syntactically eligible for an
explicit inline bounded RETRY; STEP has no RETRY and RETRY cannot be referenced.
One ACTION invocation makes at most 1 + LIMIT attempts and ends retrying on the
first successful attempt. The RETRY block declares the sole budget and its
selected core.retry handler authorizes additional attempts. Omitted WHEN is
TRUE and omitted DELAY is DURATION(0, unit.second). A FALSE WHEN before the count
is consumed preserves the preceding failure without exhaustion. A proven
pre_effect attempt with no effects may be tried again when LIMIT and WHEN
permit it. After known applied or partial effects,
another attempt additionally requires exact evidence that repetition,
resumption, or reconciliation cannot duplicate a non-idempotent effect or exceed
the wrapped action's authority, scope, and post-state. Indeterminate phase or
effects prohibit another attempt until reconciled. Missing proof, UNKNOWN proof,
and proof that retry is unsafe use error.required.missing,
error.value.unknown, and error.operation.precondition respectively.
error.retry.exhausted applies only after exactly 1 + LIMIT attempts were actually
made and all failed; a safety-blocked unmade attempt is not exhaustion. All
attempt results and diagnostics remain as ordered evidence. The exact count,
condition, safety, and delay order is in
08_PHASE_SEQUENCE_STEP_BRANCH_LOOP_RETRY_AND_CONCURRENCY.txt.
